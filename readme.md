# [Directional Positioning]Direct Positioning Method for Non-circular Signals Based on Sparse Bayesian Inference and Grid Refinement Strategy [with MATLAB Code]

Abstract: This study investigates a Direct Point-Detection (DPD) algorithm based on Out-of-Grid Sparse Bayesian Inference (OGSBI). Existing DPD algorithms based on sparse Bayesian inference face challenges such as excessive computational demands and the neglect of non-circular (NC) signals. To address these limitations, we propose an enhanced OGSBI-based DPD algorithm that is suitable for multiple NC signal sources. By leveraging the conjugate information of non-circular signals, we extend the dimensionality of the data matrix, significantly enhancing the positioning performance. Additionally, we design a grid refinement strategy to reduce computation, which initially determines the approximate location of the signal sources through a preliminary search and then employs a denser grid for fine-grained positioning. We also derive the computational complexity and Cramér-Rao lower bound (CRLB), providing a comprehensive analysis of the proposed algorithm. Numerical simulation results demonstrate that the proposed algorithm offers advantages in both positioning accuracy and computational efficiency.
Introduction: The localization of passive signal sources has received widespread attention in various fields such as vehicle technology, underwater acoustics engineering, and wireless communications. Traditional two-step localization methods require computing intermediate parameters like Angle of Arrival (AOA), Time of Arrival (TOA), and Time Difference of Arrival (TDOA), followed by spatial geometric relationships to determine the location of the signal source. However, this two-step method has two main drawbacks: firstly, it overlooks the inherent constraints imposed by measurements from the same transmitter; secondly, when data association is conducted across geographically dispersed base stations, there are challenges in achieving precise correspondence between estimated parameters and actual signal sources.
To address the limitations mentioned above, Anthony J. Weiss first introduced the concept of Direct Point-Detection (DPD). In the DPD architecture, the data fusion center does not need to perform data association but directly estimates the source position using the raw output from the transmitter. Moreover, especially in low signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) scenarios, the performance of direct positioning methods surpasses that of two-step positioning techniques. Given the potential of direct positioning algorithms in enhancing both positioning accuracy and robustness, related research has expanded to include various scenarios involving different types of signals. Particularly in modern communication systems, non-circular complex signals such as Multi-Aliased Modulation (MASK), Binary Phase Shift Keying (BPSK), and Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing (OFDM) modulated signals are more prevalent than circular ones. Research has demonstrated that leveraging the non-conjugate statistical characteristics of non-circular signals can enhance the performance of direct positioning methods.
Compared to traditional direct positioning methods, algorithms based on sparse signal representation (SSR) have higher precision and stronger robustness in scenarios with limited number of snapshots and fewer base stations. As a typical category of SSR-based methods, algorithms based on norms (0<≤1) have been extensively studied for direction of arrival (DOA) estimation and have subsequently been effectively applied to direct positioning. Although these norm-based algorithms exhibit excellent performance under low SNR and demonstrate stronger capabilities in handling coherent signals, they inevitably face some challenges, such as misalignment between global minimum and the most sparse solution (structural error), multiple suboptimal local minima (convergence error), and difficulties in tuning regularization parameters.
Simultaneously, the emergence of Relevance Vector Machines (RVM) has propelled the development of Sparse Bayesian Learning (SBL) in the field of signal processing. Sparse Bayesian learning ensures the acquisition of the most sparse solution at the global minimum (i.e., no structural error), with fewer local minima. Compared to norm-based algorithms, these characteristics of Sparse Bayesian learning contribute to enhancing signal restoration performance. Building on this, Wipf extends the Sparse Bayesian Learning model to the Multi-Measurement Vector (MMV) scenario and introduces the MSBL algorithm. The T-SBL algorithm leverages the specific temporal structure of the signal matrix by employing block sparse Bayesian learning to convert the MMV model into a Single-Measurement Vector (SMV) model. Additionally, Zhang utilizes intra-block correlation to enhance recovery performance and employs boundary optimization methods to expedite the iterative process. Recent studies have also extended Sparse Bayesian learning techniques to direct positioning scenarios: Chen et al. derived models based on AOA/TDOA-AOA within the framework of Sparse Bayesian Learning, demonstrating that their positioning accuracy surpasses traditional direct positioning methods.
In real-world scenarios, the actual signal source location is continuously changing, while candidate grid points (GPs) are discrete. The signal source parameters may not match the set of grid points, leading to grid mismatch issues. Although finer grid discretization can alleviate this issue, it incurs high computational costs. To address this challenge, a maximum a posteriori (MAP) criterion is used to maximize the posterior probability density function of unknown parameters, followed by an expectation-maximization (EM) algorithm to iteratively solve the highly non-convex cost function. Numerous studies have demonstrated that this method significantly improves computational efficiency. However, increasing the grid point density can exacerbate the correlation between the two columns of the basis vectors, thereby violating the coherence condition. Yang proposed an alternative solution, the Orthogonal Grid Sparse Bayesian Inference (OGSBI) algorithm, which utilizes sparse Bayesian inference to estimate the deviation between the true arrival angle and its nearest grid point through first-order Taylor expansion. Based on this idea, a series of grid evolution algorithms aimed at reducing computational complexity emerged. In the field of direct positioning, the OffG-SBI-DPD algorithm achieves indoor super-resolution direct positioning under multipath environments based on the orthogonal grid sparse Bayesian inference model.
As per our understanding, current research on direct positioning methods based on sparse Bayesian inference predominantly focuses on circular signal sources, overlooking the potential advantages of non-circular signals. Additionally, these methods demand high computational complexity for direct positioning, leading to longer processing times for source location. Therefore, exploring grid refinement strategies aimed at accelerating the direct positioning computation process remains a direction that warrants further investigation.
Accordingly, this study aims to address these limitations by proposing a direct positioning algorithm based on grid refinement strategy and non-circular signal for the off-grid sparse Bayesian inference. The main contributions of this study are summarized as follows:
1  
The proposed algorithm utilizes the characteristics of non-circular signals. Since non-circular signals have non-zero elliptical covariance, the algorithm concatenates the received signal and its conjugate to increase the dimension of the data matrix, effectively doubling the number of sensors, thereby significantly improving estimation accuracy.
2  
A grid refinement strategy is proposed to avoid updating a large number of irrelevant grid points during the iterative process of sparse Bayesian learning. Additionally, singular value decomposition (SVD) technology is employed for data dimensionality reduction. Performance analysis and numerical simulation results indicate that this strategy can significantly reduce computational complexity.
3  
The proposed method uses first-order Taylor expansion to linearly approximate the deviation from the true signal source to the nearest grid point, effectively solving the grid mismatch problem.

3. Sparse Bayesian Inference Positioning Based on Grid Refinement Strategy
3.2 Grid refinement strategy under two-dimensional conditions in sparse Bayesian inference, only the grid points corresponding to specific spectral peaks need to be focused on, and the continuous updating of other grid points will cause redundant computational work. Influenced by the grid refinement method in angle estimation, it is extended from one dimension to two dimensions. The core idea of this strategy is to first determine the approximate region where the related spectral peak is located, and then conduct a fine search within that specific region. The specific steps are as follows:
1  
Initialize the number of iterations, divide the interested area (square region) into uniformly spaced larger grid intervals, estimate the power spectrum for each candidate grid point, and select the location corresponding to the top-ranked spectral peak as the center point for fine segmentation.
2  
Perform grid refinement for each center point, defining candidate regions around the center point coordinates with a specific length as the half-side length and setting the interval between adjacent grid points.
3  
Check whether the grid precision meets the requirements, if it does, terminate the process, otherwise proceed to the next step.
4  
Increase the number of iterations, determine the position of the maximum power spectral peak as the center point, divide a new square area, re-set parameters and refine the candidate areas.
When generating new grid points, some parameters remain unchanged while others are reassigned, and there are specific settings for the prior distribution of sparse Bayesian inference. The setting of the initial grid interval is crucial, and it needs to be reasonably selected to avoid situations where the number of signal sources is not recognized or the grid is too dense.

Conclusion This paper proposes an enhanced direct positioning algorithm based on grid-free sparse Bayesian inference, suitable for multiple non-circular signal sources. The algorithm outperforms traditional methods based on sparse Bayesian inference in terms of both positioning accuracy and computational speed. By leveraging the characteristics of non-circular signal sources, it effectively doubles the array aperture, thereby enhancing positioning performance.
